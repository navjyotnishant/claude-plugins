## What makes these work

**Lead with the single most surprising verified finding, not a summary of the argument.** The first two lines are all most people see before "see more", so they carry the entire post. A summary of your thesis is not a hook; a fact that makes someone stop is.

The best hook is usually something concrete and slightly strange that the reader would not have guessed. A study that could not be completed. A postmortem sentence that contradicts the metric. A number that runs the opposite direction to everyone's assumption.

**Keep it short.** Roughly 600 to 900 characters. Longer posts get skimmed, and skimmed posts do not get clicks.

**Ground it in the author's own experience** if they have relevant background. One line is enough. It answers "why is this person writing about this", which every reader asks silently.

**Close with a specific ask and a link pointer.** "Would love to hear your thoughts" is the phrase that makes a post look like every other post. "If you've measured any of this at your organization, I'd like to hear about it" is better because it names who should reply.

Note that LinkedIn suppresses reach on posts containing outbound links, so tell the author to put the article link in the first comment and end the post with "Article link in the comments."

## Voice

Read the author's writing style profile if one exists (commonly `my-writing-style`) and follow its hard rules exactly. Punctuation preferences in particular tend to be absolute.

Use contractions if the author does. Avoid rule-of-three lists, "not X, not Y" constructions, and uniformly balanced sentences. These read as machine-written and people scroll past them.

## Output

Give the post as a single block the author can paste, then note anything they should verify or adjust. Do not add commentary inside the post itself.
